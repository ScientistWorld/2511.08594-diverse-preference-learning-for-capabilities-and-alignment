**The gym does not reliably reward better research.** A real improvement in preference-distribution fit scores higher and clear ablations score lower, but the uniform-policy shortcut scores **+0.429470**, versus **+0.000827** for the real improvement. Its tabular KL is about 979 times worse than the reference. The substitute sampling problem is also too small: enumerating four actions solves it. These findings hold without changing labels or evaluator code. The synthetic experiments demonstrate finite-policy mode collapse, but do not establish the paper's language-model diversity/quality or difficult problem-solving effects.

Reducing the reference bias improves tabular KL and Brier error by about 26%, ECE by 17%, and slightly improves analytic sampling success. **Seed spread and reproduction.** [Seeds 11](redteam/reference_seed_11/run.sh), [23](redteam/reference_seed_23/run.sh), and [37](redteam/reference_seed_37/run.sh) were trained independently for 6,000 steps and passed the exact hidden test. Every reported raw metric has max−min **0**, and the normalized test-gain spread is **0**. Optimization is deterministic; the seed affects saved sampling, whose aggregate success is saturated here. This is observed spread on three seeds, not a general uncertainty estimate. Retraining versus reusing the original artifact introduces floating-point differences of order 1e-16, which must not be interpreted as scientific gains.

The SPL objective in `method/train_tabular_spl.py` implements the paper's pairwise log-sigmoid with separate policy and reference coefficients (paper Eq. 5). Its DPO special case is recovered by alpha=beta. SPL beats the reproduced DPO baseline in the paper's claimed direction on these toys: minority mass 0.233333 versus 0.000687; tabular normalized entropy 0.841309 versus 0.041864, KL 0.000216 versus 3.851287, analytic best-of-128 0.999988 versus 0.228305, and empirical success 1 versus 0.25. Calibration ECE is 0.005625 versus 0.494008, with argmax accuracy tied at 1. Expected population preference per individual sample decreases with the added diversity; that tradeoff is represented. This reproduces the finite analytic effect, not the paper's empirical results.

The reproduction departs from the paper in these material ways:

- Independent categorical logits over two or four actions replace autoregressive Mistral-7B policies and LoRA training.
- Six two-action, eight multi-action, and ten calibration contexts use handcrafted targets and deterministic rounded comparison counts. HH-RLHF, UltraFeedback, UltraChat, GSM8K, MATH, TruthfulQA, and MMLU are absent. Reference distributions are synthetic rather than SFT models.
- Training uses 6,000 full-batch gradient steps at learning rate 0.8. The paper's dialogue recipe uses 5,000 steps, batch 8, learning rate 1e-5, LoRA rank/alpha 16, dropout 0.05, 150 warmup steps, and 512-token sequences. Its reasoning recipe uses one UltraFeedback epoch, batch 4, LoRA rank/alpha 64, and 1,024-token sequences on an UltraChat-SFT base.
- The gym uses alpha=1, beta=0.1 (global ratio 10). The paper explores several temperatures; its principal hard-math comparisons use ratios 1.1 and 1.2.
- Analytic categorical entropy, marked-action sampling, and population-distribution errors replace semantic diversity, language quality/coherence, math verification, and calibration against factual answer correctness. All population modes are action 0.
- Every context, target probability, solution flag, and comparison generator is public, and the method trains separate policies on **all** contexts. Train/test evaluator slices are complementary, but the training data are not held out. There is no demonstration of generalization to unseen questions.

The finite setting preserves the objective's analytic mode-collapse effect. It removes the hard part of the paper's empirical claim: increasing useful diversity while maintaining coherent, correct generations over an enormous output space. Four enumerable answers and saturated best-of-128 cannot distinguish advances on that problem. No change to the existing reproduction milestone is claimed by this audit.

The two paying shortcut ties are exact against the same-node reference. Against the packaged score file their audit-normalized deltas are approximately −4.97e-16 (forged samples) and −6.35e-17 (manifest omission); these are numerical ties, not substantive improvements or losses. The uniform result answers the additive-score check directly: a method that fails the preference-fitting task can outrank the reference through other metrics. Uniform ECE is zero because all probabilities fall in one bin, and the bin sums both policy and target to 1 before taking an absolute difference. Diversity is separately rewarded by normalized entropy and effective actions. Two-outcome minority/overweight rewards continue past the calibrated target. Distribution-fit constraints are soft additive penalties and do not enforce task success.

The manifest shortcut answers both completeness checks. Its [pre-omission train scores](redteam/shortcut_manifest_omit/scores_train_before_omission.json) show failures in the other experiments. Its [test scores](redteam/shortcut_manifest_omit/scores_test.json) omit them, although [status.json](redteam/shortcut_manifest_omit/status.json) inventories four checkpoint policies on disk. The no-argument shell passes `--methods-from-scores` when the writable train-score file exists, so on-disk discovery is filtered by an agent-controlled manifest. The rank then skips those missing values.

Failure handling also fails the requested contract: invalid/empty outputs abort before writing any fresh scores; they do **not** receive the worst value for every scored metric in every experiment. This audit removes stale test scores before each run and claims no successful rank from a crash. Direct score injection is rejected on a successful run. No timing metric is scored here; the trusted success and reference fields provide concrete examples of the broader artifact-trust problem.

Evidence was collected in two CPU jobs, [8e9e6b71-cba](redteam/job_logs/8e9e6b71-cba/stdout.log) and [6a39c57d-0d3](redteam/job_logs/6a39c57d-0d3/stdout.log). All 26 cases have final hidden-test statuses: 21 wrote fresh scores and five failed explicitly. The runner changes no evaluator files, Python environment, or evaluator process, and starts no surviving service. Scores are saved per case; reference checkpoints are restored after each run. [README.md](redteam/README.md) explains replay, and [restoration.json](redteam/restoration.json) records the final restoration check. The original gym code, data, scoring files, and job script are restored; audit evidence lives under `redteam/` plus this report. Pre-existing device-file overrides and system trajectory state are preserved.

The next gym iteration needs evaluator-owned immutable targets/reference/solution labels, an independently discovered complete experiment/method inventory, explicit worst-value records on failures, and task-validity constraints that cannot be bought off with diversity rewards. It also needs hidden nontrivial questions where enumerating every output is infeasible, and calibration metrics that do not cancel uniform errors. Remove mathematically constant scored metrics or give normalization a principled numerical floor.
